How to Repair a Leaky Bathroom Faucet in Methuen

A dripping bathroom faucet almost always comes down to one worn part inside the handle. Two-handle faucets usually need a new rubber washer or seat. Single-handle faucets usually need a new cartridge or O-rings. The repair takes about an hour with a few basic hand tools.

Shut the small valves under the sink, plug the drain so nothing falls in, open the faucet to relieve pressure, then take the handle apart and compare the parts with replacements from the store. If the faucet body is corroded, the drip comes from the base, or the shutoff valves will not close, stop and call (603) 815-9179.

Step zero

Start by naming the faucet you have

The fix depends on the faucet's guts, and bathroom sinks tend to come in four designs. Look at the handles and pull one off if you have to. You will recognize the type in under a minute.

Before you open it

Gather tools and shut the water off

Tools and supplies

Most of this is already in a drawer.

  • Adjustable wrench and slip-joint pliers
  • Phillips and flat screwdrivers
  • Hex key set for set-screw handles
  • Penetrating oil and a rag for stuck nuts
  • Replacement washers, O-rings or cartridge (bring the old part to match it)
  • Plumber's grease for rubber seals
  • A towel or washcloth to plug the drain

Shut it off first, then confirm

Turn each small oval-handled stop valve under the sink clockwise until snug. Open the faucet to bleed pressure and check that the flow stops. If a stop valve will not turn or keeps leaking, do not force it. Close the house main instead and call a plumber.

Put a towel in the basin and cover the drain. A tiny screw or retaining clip falls into a drain faster than you can catch it.

Repair

Repairing the drip, step by step

1

Remove the handle

Pop off the decorative cap with a small flat screwdriver. Back out the screw or loosen the set screw with a hex key. Wiggle the handle up and off.

2

Pull the cartridge or stem

Unscrew the packing nut or retaining clip. For compression faucets, the stem unthreads. For cartridge faucets, pull the cartridge straight up, noting how it faces so the new one goes in the same way.

3

Inspect the rubber

Look at the washer, seat and O-rings. Flattened, split or hardened rubber is your answer. A gritty, scratched seat can be dressed with a seat tool, or the whole faucet may be worn out.

4

Replace and lubricate

Fit the new washer or O-rings, add a thin film of plumber's grease and reinstall the part. Do not overtighten a retaining nut, since brass threads strip quickly.

5

Reassemble and test slowly

Open the faucet handle, turn the stop valves back on a quarter turn at a time and check for drips at the base and under the sink. Let the water run for a minute to flush debris.

A seized handle or a stop valve that spins freely is a sign to stop. Call (603) 815-9179 and we can schedule a same-day visit when available.

Reading the leak

Which part to blame, by symptom

Drip from the spout after shutoff

The washer, seat or cartridge is no longer sealing. This is the most common repair.

Water at the base of the handle

The O-rings on the stem or cartridge have worn. Replace them as a set.

Water around the faucet base on the deck

A deck gasket or the mounting hardware has loosened, or a supply line is seeping below. Dry it and watch where it returns.

Handle is stiff or sticky

Mineral buildup or dried grease inside the cartridge. A cleaning and fresh grease may be enough.

If the wet spot keeps returning and you cannot tell if it is the faucet or the supply, we can trace it with our leak detection equipment before anyone cuts into a vanity or wall.

Decide

DIY or call a plumber

Good DIY candidates

  • A steady drip from one spout
  • A leak around one handle
  • A single washer, O-ring or cartridge swap
  • Faucets less than two decades old with parts still sold

Better left to a licensed plumber

  • Corroded or crumbling shutoff valves
  • A faucet that needs to be replaced and re-fitted
  • Leaks inside the vanity or the wall
  • Discolored or pitted brass that will not seal again

If you decide the whole faucet is the problem, our faucet repair team can fix or replace it and test every connection. Small repairs on a leaky faucet generally do not require a permit in Massachusetts, but a licensed plumber should handle anything involving new piping.

Cartridge and seat kits are made to each brand's own standards, so match the number on the manufacturer's parts sheet before you buy. New piping is covered by 248 CMR, which is why that stays with a licensed plumber.

Avoid

What not to do

  • Do not crank on a handle that will not turn. Brass and chrome break.
  • Do not use plumber's tape on a faucet cartridge. It is for threaded pipe joints.
  • Do not force a rounded nut with locking pliers over and over. Use penetrating oil and give it time.
  • Do not leave the supply on while you disassemble anything.
  • Do not ignore a drip you have already had for months. The seat wears faster the longer it leaks.
Cost factors

What affects the price

The part

Washers and O-rings are cheap and quick. A full cartridge or a sink faucet replacement costs more.

Access

A pedestal sink, narrow vanity or corroded handle slows the work.

Shutoff valve condition

Failed stop valves add a valve replacement to the job.

Faucet age and availability

Discontinued models may need a replacement faucet rather than a rebuild.
