Leaking Kitchen Faucet Repair in Methuen, MA

Start by finding where the water shows up, because the location names the failed part. A drip from the spout means a worn cartridge, washer or seat. Water at the handle means worn O-rings. A wet base or a puddle in the cabinet usually means a base gasket, a loose hose connection or a cracked supply line.

Single-handle faucets are repaired by swapping the cartridge. Two-handle faucets need a new washer or stem on the leaking side. A pull-down sprayer that drips needs a new hose or quick-connect. When the faucet body is cracked, or you see water below the sink you cannot trace, call (603) 815-9179.

Trace it first

Where is the water coming from?

From the spout tip

Water drips after the handle is off. The internal seal, cartridge or seat is worn.

Around the handle

Water runs down the neck when the faucet is on. The O-rings on the stem or cartridge are done.

At the swivel or spout base

A ring of water around the spout means the spout O-rings, usually two, have flattened.

Under the sink

Dampness on the cabinet floor can be the supply lines, the stop valves, the pull-down hose connection or the sink drain. Dry everything and watch where it returns.

From the sprayer

A dripping spray head or a hose that weeps at its coupling is a different repair from a cartridge job.

Compare

Single-handle versus two-handle: the repair differs

Single-handle faucet

  • One cartridge controls hot, cold and flow
  • Replace the cartridge or the spout O-rings
  • Remove the handle, retaining clip and cartridge, noting its orientation
  • Cartridge types vary by maker, so take the old one to match it

Two-handle faucet

  • Each side has its own stem, washer or cartridge
  • Only the leaking side usually needs work
  • Replace the washer and seat, or the whole stem
  • Check the other side for wear, since it is the same age

Walkthrough

Replacing a cartridge in a single-handle faucet

1

Shut off supply

Close both stop valves under the sink. Open the faucet in the middle position to confirm it stops flowing.

2

Take off the handle and cap

Look for a set screw under a decorative cap or on the back of the lever. Remove the handle and any dome or sleeve under it.

3

Release the cartridge

Remove the retaining clip or nut. Grip the cartridge stem and pull it straight up, twisting gently if it sticks.

4

Match and fit

Compare the old and new cartridges side by side. Align the tabs, push the new one fully in, and replace the clip.

5

Test

Reassemble, open the stop valves a little at a time, and run both hot and cold. Check under the sink with a dry paper towel.

The extras

Pull-down hose, sprayer and aerator problems

Check these before blaming the cartridge

  • Aerator: unscrew it and rinse out grit. A clogged aerator makes weak flow and can send water sideways.
  • Pull-down hose: look for a kink, a worn weight or a loose quick-connect under the sink.
  • Spray head: if it drips after shutoff, the check valve or the diverter inside has worn.
  • Hose fittings: hand-tighten a quarter turn past snug. Do not use thread tape on compression fittings.
  • Supply lines: replace braided lines that show rust, bulges or kinks.

If you find a cracked body or a corroded base, the faucet should be replaced, not rebuilt. Our plumbing installation team can fit a new faucet and test the supply lines and stop valves at the same time.

Know when

DIY, call, or stop right now

Fine to try yourself

  • Aerator cleaning
  • Cartridge or O-ring swap with the stop valves working
  • Replacing a supply line with a matched fitting
  • Tightening a hose or a mounting nut

Call a licensed plumber

  • Stop valves that will not close
  • Leaks below the sink that you cannot trace
  • A faucet that needs replacing in a granite or quartz top
  • Any sign of mold or soft cabinet floor

Water and electricity

If the leak is near a garbage disposal switch, an outlet or a dishwasher, turn off those circuits at the breaker before you reach under the sink. Stay out of standing water near live outlets.

Cost factors

What affects the price

Part cost

Aerators and O-rings are minor. A branded cartridge or a pull-down hose assembly costs more.

Faucet design

Pull-down and touchless faucets take longer to service than basic ones.

Sink and cabinet access

A deep, crowded cabinet slows removal and testing.

Repair versus replacement

When parts are obsolete, the cost shifts to a new faucet plus installation.

Reading Methuen's annual water report, and who to call about odd usage

The Methuen Water Department publishes a water quality report every year. The 2023 report lists pH, alkalinity, sodium and dissolved solids for the water leaving the plant, and the department treats with a blended phosphate corrosion inhibitor. The latest posted report is the 2025 edition, with earlier years in the archive on the city site.

To use it for your faucet, look for pH and alkalinity, then compare what you see on your fixtures. White crust at the aerator or a stiff handle suggests scale. The report does not hand you a verdict on your tap, so test it yourself or ask the department what your area reads.

A leaking faucet adds to your water use all day. If your bill jumps with no change in habits, call the Methuen Water Department billing line at 978-983-8555. If water is coming from the street side or the meter, use the 24-hour water emergency line at 978-983-8855.
