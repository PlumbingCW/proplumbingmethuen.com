Emergency Water Line Repair in Methuen, MA

Water service line repair and replacement is work on the buried pipe that carries city water from the main in the street to your house. If that line breaks, you can lose pressure, flood a yard, or see a basement fill. Our licensed plumbers are on 24/7 emergency dispatch at (603) 815-9179.

Water line leaking right now: your first moves

A water line leak outside is different from a leak at a faucet. The water keeps coming from the street at full pressure until you shut it off. Start with the shutoff where the line enters your house.

1

Close the main shutoff

Turn the main valve in the basement clockwise to stop water at the house. If it will not turn, do not force it.

2

Call the water emergency line if the valve fails

If the house valve is stuck or the leak is on the street side of the meter, call the Methuen Water Department 24-hour water emergency line at 978-983-8855 to ask about a shutoff at the street.

3

Keep clear of flooded areas

If water is entering the basement, switch off breakers only if you can reach the panel dry. Do not wade into standing water near electrical equipment.

4

Call us

Dial (603) 815-9179. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and after-hours calls go to the same 24/7 dispatch.

Symptoms

Signs your service line is leaking

Meter moving with everything off

Check the meter after shutting off all fixtures. A moving dial points to a leak in the house or the line.

Wet or sunken patch in the lawn

A soggy or unusually green spot, a small sinkhole, or water at the surface in dry weather suggests a buried leak.

Sudden drop in water pressure

A broken line loses water before it reaches the house, so showers and taps weaken.

Dirty or discolored water

When a line breaks and pressure drops, soil and debris can enter. Run the cold tap and call if it doesn't clear.

Water at the foundation

Moisture at the wall where the line enters can mean the leak is close to the house.

If you spot more than one, assume the line is the problem until a test says otherwise. We use acoustic listening along the line to find it. See our leak detection page for how that works.

Pressure

Low water pressure all through the house

Low pressure at every fixture usually points to the main, not a single faucet. If only one fixture is weak, look at the aerator or valve. If all of them are weak, the cause could be a partly closed main valve, a leak, a failing pressure regulator, or a corroded line.

Try this: open a cold tap at the lowest point and compare it with one upstairs. Then check the main valve is fully open. If nothing changes, a service line leak or a clogged older pipe may be the cause. Old galvanized pipe can narrow inside as it rusts, which reduces flow without any leak.

Quick checks before you call

  • Is the main valve fully open?
  • Are the neighbors also seeing low pressure? If yes, call the water department.
  • Is the problem on hot water only? That points to the water heater.
  • Is the meter moving with all fixtures off?

If pressure is the only symptom and there is no leak, we can check the pressure regulator and test the line. Water heater issues are covered under water heater repair.

Local notes

Methuen water and what it means for old lines

Methuen's water comes from the Merrimack River through a single intake and filtration plant. The Methuen Water Department reports about 200 miles of water main. In its annual report it says homes built before 1940 may have lead service lines, and that it is still replacing leftover lead goosenecks.

The city also publishes a service line inventory map. If your house is older, check it or ask the department what material your line is. If it is lead, galvanized, or unknown, tell us before we dig, because a replacement is the moment to fix it.

The state building code table uses 48 inches as frost depth. Many service lines sit below that depth, but an older or shallow line is a risk in a hard freeze. If you see a frozen service line, our burst pipe repair page covers thawing safely.

Main shutoff valve problems

A main shutoff that will not close, leaks at the stem, or breaks when you turn it is its own problem. It is the valve you rely on in a flood. Old gate valves seize with age, and the handle can snap.

Do not use a wrench for extra force. Call us and we can replace it with a full-port ball valve, which closes with a quarter turn. If the street shutoff is also needed, we coordinate with the water department's emergency line at 978-983-8855.

Before anyone digs

Call 811 before any excavation so underground utilities get marked. Gas, electric, and communications lines can run near your water line. Digging without marking is a safety hazard and can be expensive.

A new service line or a relocated main may also touch on plumbing installation work, such as moving the meter or valves. In Massachusetts, plumbing permits are issued to licensed plumbers, and our plumbers handle that when it applies.
