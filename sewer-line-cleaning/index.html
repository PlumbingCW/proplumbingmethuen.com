Sewer backup: what to do first

1

Stop all water

No flushing, showers, dishwasher or laundry. Every drop adds to the problem.

2

Keep out of the water

Sewage carries bacteria. Keep children and pets away, and do not touch it barehanded.

3

Turn off power if water is near electric

If water is near outlets, a furnace or a panel, shut off the breaker only if you can reach it without standing in water. Otherwise leave the room and call.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Signs of a main line clog

Several fixtures at once

A toilet, shower and sink all draining poorly means the shared main drain is the likely problem.

Floor drain activity

Water or gurgling in a basement floor drain is often the first sign the main line is blocked.

Flushing sends water elsewhere

If a flush makes the tub bubble or fill, the water has nowhere to go but out the lowest opening.

Bad smell near the cleanout

Sewer odor in the basement without any visible water can mean the line is partly blocked.

Your pipe or the city main: telling them apart

Part of Methuen's sewer system is combined, carrying household wastewater and storm runoff in the same pipe, and the flow is treated at the Greater Lawrence Sanitary District plant in North Andover. In heavy rain, a combined system can get overloaded, and flow can push back toward low basements.

That is why it matters to know where your problem starts. A clog in your own lateral is a plumbing job. A backed-up city main is something the city has to address.

If the cleanout shows water and a cable clears the line, it was yours. If the line is clear and the backup comes only with rain, call the city. We will tell you honestly which one we think it is, and we do not assign blame either way.

Heavy rain, surcharge and backwater

During intense storms, water levels in the sewer rise. If your basement drain sits lower than the sewer, the flow can reverse. A backwater valve on the building drain is one way to stop that, and we can look at whether one makes sense for your home.

Methuen has places that flood in storms, including the Bloody Brook and Jackson Street corridor, where city reports describe localized flash flooding two to three times a year. If you live in a low area near the Spicket River, a storm backup may not mean your pipe is clogged. See our sump pump repair page if your basement also takes on groundwater.

Where is the cleanout?

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives direct access to the main drain. Most homes have one in the basement near where the main drain exits the foundation, or outside close to the house wall. It is often 3 or 4 inches across, with a square or round cap.

Do not open a cleanout under pressure. If the line is full, removing the cap releases sewage. Let a plumber do it with a plan to contain the flow.

Cabling or jetting a sewer line

Cabling

  • Drills through the clog
  • Good for a first-time stoppage
  • Works through a standard cleanout
  • May not clear grease on the walls

Hydro jetting

  • Scours the pipe clean
  • Best for grease and repeat clogs
  • Needs pipe in good shape
  • Camera look recommended first

We pick the method after seeing what the line is doing. Read more on hydro jetting if your main has clogged more than once.

Roots in the sewer line

Roots find tiny gaps in joints and grow into the pipe looking for water. A root mass can stop flow completely. Clay and older cast iron are the usual targets. A cutting head can clear them, but they come back unless the joint is repaired or the pipe is relined.

Prevention that actually works

  • Keep wipes, even ones labeled flushable, out of the toilet
  • Put grease in a can for the trash, not in the sink
  • Schedule a camera check if you have had more than one backup
  • Know where your cleanout is and keep it accessible
  • Keep large trees from being planted over the line

Floor drain and flooded basement safety

Until we arrive

  • Wear boots and gloves if you must enter, and wash hands thoroughly
  • Do not run a wet vacuum on sewage
  • Do not mix cleaning chemicals, especially bleach and ammonia
  • Photograph the damage before you move anything
  • Open windows for ventilation if safe to do so

If your system has been flooded with sewage, discard porous items that got soaked, such as carpet and cardboard. For ongoing groundwater or seepage, the basement waterproofing page explains what can be done.
