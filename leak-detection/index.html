Active leak

Water is leaking right now: what to do before we arrive

1

Close the main shutoff

Find the main valve where the water line enters the house, usually in the basement near the meter. Turn it clockwise until it stops. A ball valve with a lever closes with a quarter turn.

2

Cut power near the water

If water is dripping from a ceiling light, soaking an outlet, or pooling near the panel, switch off that circuit at the breaker. Do not stand in water to do it. If the panel itself is wet, stay out and call us and the electric utility.

3

Open a low faucet to drain the lines

With the main closed, open a tap on the lowest floor to relieve pressure. That slows the leak while you wait.

4

Contain and document

Move rugs and furniture, set out buckets, and take photos of the damage and the source. Your insurance company will ask for them.

5

Call the 24/7 line

Dial (603) 815-9179 and describe what you see and where. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and after-hours calls go to the same dispatch.

Smell gas or see sparks?

Leave the building and call 911 and the gas utility first. A water leak is never worth staying in a space with a gas smell or an electrical hazard.

Meter and high bill

The water meter keeps moving with everything turned off

A meter that creeps forward when every tap, appliance, and ice maker is idle means water is going somewhere. The same goes for a bill that jumped with no change in how your household lives. Both are classic signs of a hidden leak on the house side of the meter.

A running toilet is the most common cause and costs nothing to rule out first. After that, we look at supply lines, the water heater connections, and the buried service line.

A five-minute meter check you can do yourself

Make sure nobody is using water, then read the meter and write down the number. Look for the small triangle or dial that spins when any flow passes through. If it turns with everything off, there is a leak.

Wait 30 minutes without using water and read it again. If the number moved, close the main shutoff and check the meter once more. A meter that stops after you close your main points to a leak inside the house or in the line to it. If it keeps moving with your main closed, the problem may be upstream and the water department should hear about it.

Ceilings and walls

A brown stain on the ceiling or damp patches on the wall

Water travels. A stain on the dining room ceiling may start at a bathroom drain two feet over, or at a supply line that runs along a joist before it drips. The wet spot is where the water gave up, not where it began.

Ceiling and wall leaks also change fast. A small stain can turn into a sagging, bulging panel within hours, and wet drywall can hold a lot of weight. If the ceiling is bowing, keep everyone out from under it. Any repair that follows a leak find is done to the Massachusetts plumbing code (248 CMR) standards by a licensed plumber.

Stain with a ring that grows

Rings that get darker and wider after a shower or a dishwasher cycle point to a drain or supply line above the stain.

Paint bubbling or peeling

Moisture behind paint pushes it off the surface. It often shows up months before drywall softens.

Musty smell with no visible water

A smell in a closet or room corner can mean a slow wall leak. A moisture meter reads it without cutting anything open.

Sound of running water in a quiet house

Hissing or trickling inside a wall when nothing is on is worth a call. Acoustic gear can pick up that sound at the pipe.

The plumber uses a moisture meter and thermal imaging to map the wet area, then confirm the source before any cutting. Often the opening we need is a small patch you can repaint, not a whole wall.

Under the house

Damp basement, musty crawlspace, or a floor that feels soft

New England homes sit on basements and crawlspaces, so a lot of plumbing hangs where you can see it, and a lot is hidden behind finished ceilings. That is good news for finding leaks and bad news for ignoring them. A slow drip on a basement supply line can run for months before it shows upstairs.

Look for rust bloom on pipe fittings, white mineral crust on copper joints, drips on the underside of the kitchen or bathroom floor, and wet insulation. In a crawlspace, check for a wet dirt floor under the bathroom, and for a smell that wasn't there last season.

Wet from a leak, or wet from the ground?

Not every wet basement is a plumbing leak. The Methuen DPW reports flash flooding two to three times a year in the Bloody Brook and Jackson Street corridor, and the Spicket River gauge reached 9.86 feet on March 16, 2010 against an 8.0 foot flood stage. In those areas, groundwater after heavy rain can look like a pipe problem.

The test is simple. If the floor is wet only after rain, with the meter standing still, it is groundwater. A (603) 815-9179 call can sort that out, and if it is water coming in, our sump pump repair page covers the next step. If the meter moves during dry weather, you have a leak.

What to look for under the house

  • Drips or stains on the underside of the floor above bathrooms and kitchens
  • Green or white crust on copper fittings
  • Wet or sagging insulation between joists
  • Water on the dirt floor of a crawlspace with no recent rain
  • Mold smell in a space that was dry last year
Buried lines

Water in the yard or a leak somewhere underground

A buried water service line leaks quietly. There is no stain and no drip, just a meter that runs, or a patch of lawn that stays green, soft, or wet when the rest of the yard is dry. Pressure at the faucets often drops as well. If you have a cold, soggy spot in winter, that can be the line.

The state building code table uses 48 inches as a design frost depth. Many service lines are buried below that, but an older or shallow line can still freeze. The plumber locates the leak by listening along the pipe and marking the spot before any digging. You get a clear price for the repair before work starts. For the repair itself, see our water service line repair page.

Once a leak is located, the repair follows the Massachusetts plumbing code (248 CMR). Ask the plumber how deep the new run will sit and how it will be protected in winter.

Tools and method

How we find leaks without opening the whole wall

Each instrument answers a different question. The plumber starts with the least invasive method that fits your symptoms and may combine two when the first result is unclear. Ask to see where the leak is before anyone makes a cut.

MethodWhat it doesBest for

No method is perfect on its own. Plastic pipe is quiet, noisy neighborhoods mask sound, and very small leaks can be tricky. That is why the plumber may pair methods. When a leak is in a sealed wall or under a finished floor, thermal imaging narrows where the opening has to go.

Honest answer

Slab leaks in Methuen: how likely are they?

A slab leak is a leak in a pipe that runs under or inside a concrete slab floor. Slab leaks are less common in basement homes. Additions, garages and some newer homes may be slab-on-grade.

If yours is slab-on-grade, warm spots on the floor, a hissing sound, or a meter that keeps moving with nothing running deserve a look. The plumber uses listening gear and thermal imaging to look for them, then explains your options.

Smaller cases

Yard and meter-box leaks at the curb

Yard and meter-box leaks

Standing water around the meter box can come from a fitting at the meter or from the line just past it. Do not open the box if it is flooded. If the leak is at the curb stop or on the street side, call the Methuen Water Department emergency line at 978-983-8855 for a shutoff there.

Options

Repair now or replace the section: how we decide

Spot repair

  • Best when one fitting or one short run of pipe has failed
  • Smaller opening and shorter job
  • Makes sense when the rest of the pipe is in sound condition

Replace the section

  • Best when pipe is corroded in several places or has leaked before
  • Costs more now and cuts repeat visits
  • Common with old galvanized steel or thin-wall copper

We explain both and you choose. If the leak points to a bigger issue, such as a line that keeps failing, repiping may come up. We will not push it when a repair holds. Related burst or split pipes are covered under burst pipe repair.
