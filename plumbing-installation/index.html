Gas lines and gas smells: safety first

If you smell gas, leave first

Get everyone out of the building, do not touch light switches or phones inside, and call Eversource or 911 from outside. Call a plumber after the gas utility says the area is safe. Never wait for us to arrive before leaving.

In Massachusetts, gas work is licensed work. Only a licensed gas fitter may do gas piping, and a permit is part of the job. That covers new lines for a range, dryer, fireplace or grill, and it covers repairs after a leak.

For a gas line installation or inspection, the plumber shuts off the gas, installs or changes the pipe, then pressure tests every joint before the appliance is connected. We cannot promise a response time on a gas problem, because the first call belongs to the gas utility.

Installed with the right questions asked

Water softeners and filtration

Many homeowners ask about a softener after seeing white scale on faucets or a cloudy film on glassware. Before buying anything, find out whether your water actually needs treatment. We do not state a hardness figure for Methuen. Ask the Water Department for its annual report, and test your tap with a kit.

That report is posted each year, and it lists things like sodium, which a salt-based softener would add to. A home on a low-sodium diet should factor that in. The department treats Methuen water with a phosphate corrosion inhibitor and pH adjustment, so your water may already be balanced toward protecting pipes.

Whole-house softener

Treats hard water for the whole house, with a brine tank and drain connection. Needs sizing for your household and a spot with power and a floor drain.

Carbon filter

Reduces chlorine taste and odor. It does not soften water. Filters need changing on the schedule the maker sets.

Under-sink drinking filter

Treats just one tap. Cheaper and quicker to install, and many homes start here.

Softener valve and bypass

A failing softener control valve or a missing bypass is a common reason for a service call. We can replace the valve or the whole unit.

Permits and the licensed-plumber rule

A permit must be issued before most plumbing work is installed or altered, and under Massachusetts rules (248 CMR 3.05) permits go to licensed plumbers. Minor repairs like fixing a leaky faucet are exempt.

The Methuen Inspections Division at 41 Pleasant Street, Suite 203, issues plumbing permits and can be reached at 978-983-8625. The 2022 fee schedule listed a 50 dollar minimum, 5 dollars per fixture and 50 dollars for a water heater, so check current fees. You can verify any plumber's licence on mass.gov.

You can check that any plumber you hire holds a valid licence by using the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters lookup on mass.gov, which is the standard way to verify credentials.

Shower, tub and new-construction work

A shower or tub swap looks simple, but the drain, valve depth and framing often decide the job. A new tub in a house with a slab may need floor cutting, and a walk-in shower needs the right slope.

For additions and new construction, the plumber works with the builder on sequence. Rough-in goes in before walls close, and the plumbing inspector sees it before it is covered. We do not do the tile, framing or electrical.

If you are also replacing a tank, see water heater installation for the details on that job, and if you are building out a business space, our commercial plumbing team handles that side.
