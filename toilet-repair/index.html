Urgent

Toilet overflowing or leaking at the base: act right now

An overflowing bowl puts dirty water on your floor, and a base leak can soak the subfloor for weeks before you notice. Neither one waits well.

Reach behind the toilet and close the supply valve. If the bowl is rising, lift the tank lid and push the rubber flapper down to stop more water entering. Do not flush again to see if it clears.

Water near an outlet or a ceiling below?

Water and electricity do not mix. If the leak has reached a light fixture, outlet or the ceiling of the room below, switch off that breaker only if you can reach the panel dry, and keep everyone out of the wet area.

Find the cause

Running toilet diagnosis in four steps

A toilet that hisses, refills on its own or runs for minutes after a flush wastes water every hour of the day. Most runs come down to three parts inside the tank.

1

Check the flapper

Lift the tank lid and look at the rubber seal over the drain. If it is warped, cracked or slimy, it lets water leak into the bowl and the tank keeps refilling.

2

Add dye to the tank

A few drops of food coloring in the tank, no flush for fifteen minutes. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper or seat is leaking.

3

Watch the fill valve

If water keeps rising until it spills down the overflow tube, the fill valve is not shutting off. A float set too high does the same.

4

Check the chain and float

A chain that is too tight holds the flapper open. One that is too loose can tangle. Adjust the float arm or cup if the water level sits above the overflow mark.

Flappers and fill valves are inexpensive parts, but the wrong size or brand can fail again quickly. If you are not sure which one fits, let us confirm it. A silent leak can waste a lot of water over a month, and if the dye test is clean but the meter still moves, our leak detection service checks the rest of the house.

Two different jobs

Clogged toilet or a vent problem?

A single clog is usually paper or a foreign object stuck in the trap. A plunger with a flange is the safe first try, pushed slowly so it does not slosh water over the rim.

If the toilet clears and then slows again within days, or other drains gurgle when you flush, the cause may sit further down the line. A partly blocked vent can also make a toilet flush weakly and gurgle. Those are not plunger jobs.

Local clog

  • Only this toilet is affected
  • A plunger or closet auger clears it
  • It stays clear for weeks

Deeper line or vent issue

  • Tub or sink gurgles when you flush
  • Slow flush returns days after clearing
  • More than one fixture backs up

When more than one drain backs up, we may need a camera or a cable run. See drain cleaning for what that involves, or sewer line repair if the main line is damaged.

The parts under the toilet

Wax ring, flange and supply valve repairs

The wax ring sits between the toilet and the floor flange and seals the waste path. When it fails, water or sewer gas can escape at the base. A rocking toilet wears the ring out, so loose bolts are often the root cause.

The flange is the ring bolted to the floor. If it is cracked, corroded or sits too low, a new wax ring will not seal, and the flange itself needs a repair. Shims fix a toilet that rocks on an uneven floor, and a stable toilet is what keeps a new ring working.

The supply stop valve is a small part that does a big job: it lets you isolate the toilet. Valves that drip at the stem, stick or will not close completely should be replaced. We often change the braided supply line at the same time.

Decision guide

Repair or replace the toilet?

Repair makes sense when

  • The bowl and tank are not cracked
  • The problem is a flapper, fill valve or ring
  • The toilet flushes well and fits the room

Replace makes sense when

  • The porcelain is cracked or leaking
  • You need repeated repairs on an older toilet
  • It clogs often or flushes weakly

Newer toilets use less water per flush. WaterSense is a real EPA label for fixtures that meet water-efficiency standards, and it is worth looking for when you shop.

Installation means shutting off the supply, removing the old toilet, checking the flange, setting a new wax ring, bolting the toilet down level with shims where needed and testing for leaks. Always follow the manufacturer's instructions for the model you choose.
