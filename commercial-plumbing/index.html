Emergency Commercial Plumbing in Methuen, MA

Commercial plumbing is the repair, testing and maintenance of the water, drain, waste and backflow systems inside a business building. When a restaurant kitchen floods or a retail restroom backs up after closing, the clock is running against your inventory, your health inspection and your next opening.

After-hours emergencies

Water on the floor at your business right now

A burst supply line, an overflowing floor drain or a toilet that will not stop running does not wait for business hours. If you are standing in water, the first few minutes decide how much stock, flooring and drywall you lose.

Call the 24/7 line at (603) 815-9179 and tell the dispatcher what is leaking, what floor it is on and whether anyone is hurt or any electrical gear is wet. A licensed plumber is sent as soon as one is available, and we never promise an arrival time we cannot control.

Do these things while you wait for the plumber. None of them need tools.

Find the main shutoff

Most commercial buildings have a main valve near the water meter or where the line enters the building. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If you cannot find it, tell the dispatcher.

Stop using the drains

If a floor drain or fixture is backing up, stop flushing, washing and running dishwashers. Every gallon you add has nowhere to go.

Keep people away from wet electrical areas

Water and live equipment do not mix. Switch off the breaker for a wet area only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Otherwise leave it and call.

Move what you can

Lift stock, paper records and electronics off the floor. Take photos of the damage for your insurer before you clean up.

Kitchens run hardest

Restaurants and food service

Kitchens put more load on a plumbing system than almost any other business. Fryer oil, starch and food scraps build up in drain lines and in the grease trap, and the first sign is usually a sink that drains slowly during the dinner rush or a floor drain that gurgles.

A clogged grease line left alone turns into a full backup at the worst time, usually a busy Friday. Regular line clearing and jetting cost far less than a closed kitchen.

Grease traps and the lines that serve them

Grease trap trouble often shows up first in the lines serving the trap. We clear and jet those lines so wastewater flows through, and trap pumping and disposal go to an approved hauler. How often it is needed depends on how much you cook and how big the trap is.

Ask the city which department sets grease trap rules for your kitchen. Pumped grease must go to an approved hauler, so we tell you what we find and leave disposal paperwork to the licensed hauler.

Restrooms and hidden leaks

Offices, retail and storefronts

Running or weak toilets

Commercial flush valves and tank-style toilets wear out with heavy use. A constant hiss or a toilet that needs two flushes points to a worn valve or a partial clog.

Slow restroom drains

Paper towels and wipes in a restroom line clog it fast. If more than one fixture is slow, the clog is in the shared branch.

Water stains on ceilings

A stain below a restroom or a mechanical room usually means a supply or drain leak overhead. It can run for weeks before it shows.

Pressure that drops at peak times

Low pressure on upper floors can mean a failing pressure valve, a partly closed shutoff or old corroded piping.

Commercial leak detection

If your water bill climbs and nothing looks wrong, a hidden leak may be running in a slab, wall or buried line. A quick check is to shut off every fixture and watch the meter. If it keeps moving, water is going somewhere.

We use acoustic listening gear and thermal imaging to narrow it down, but be aware that some leaks are hard to pin down, especially under a slab in a building with many tenants. We will tell you what we found, what we could not confirm and what the next step costs before we open anything. For a smaller or residential problem, see our leak detection page.

Shared lines, shared problems

Multi-unit buildings, landlords and property managers

In a multi-unit building, one clog or leak affects more than one tenant. A stack that backs up can send wastewater into the lowest unit, and a slow leak in a chase wall can damage three apartments before anyone sees it.

Property managers should know where the main shutoffs are, label them and share the location with on-site staff. That one habit shortens almost every emergency call.

Before you call about a tenant issue

Having these answers ready speeds up dispatch.

  • Which unit or floor is affected, and is the problem inside one unit or the shared line?
  • Is water still running, or have you already closed a valve?
  • Is anything electrical wet?
  • Who can give the plumber access, and is there a keyholder on site?
  • Do you need an invoice split between owner and tenant?

Annual paperwork

Backflow testing for commercial buildings

A backflow preventer keeps dirty water from flowing backward into the clean supply. Backflow devices usually need periodic testing. A failed test means the device needs a repair or replacement before it can be certified.

Ask the Methuen Inspections Division and the water department what applies to your building and your device. The 2022 fee schedule listed 30 dollars for a residential backflow device permit and 50 dollars for a commercial one, so check the current fees with the Inspections Division at 978-983-8625.

Backflow device testing should be done according to the manufacturer's guidelines and any rules your water department sets, so keep each test report on file for your building.

The low spots in your building

Floor drains, lift pumps and storm drains

A floor drain that overflows after heavy rain is often a sign that the line downstream cannot keep up. Part of Methuen's sewer system is combined, so storm flow and wastewater share pipes in some areas. In a low level, that can mean sewage rising through a floor drain, and a backwater valve may be worth asking about.

If your building uses a lift pump or sewage ejector to move waste uphill from a basement restroom, a failed pump or a stuck float causes overflow quickly. Listen for a pump that runs constantly or not at all, and call early.

Storm drain cleaning is a separate job from interior drains. Catch basins on a parking lot fill with sand and leaves, and a clogged one sends water toward your door. We can jet and clear lines we can reach, but work in the public street belongs to the city.

What local conditions mean for you

Older buildings and the Methuen water supply

Methuen's water comes only from the Merrimack River, and the city adds a phosphate corrosion inhibitor to protect pipe walls. Even so, older buildings around Broadway and the historic districts may still have galvanized steel or aging cast iron inside the walls, and those pipes narrow and fail with age.

Methuen's local historic district reviews exterior changes visible from a public way. Ask the commission before changing exterior vents or piping. For old piping that is failing throughout, a repiping plan is usually cheaper than chasing repeated leaks.

What we do and do not handle

We handle plumbing: water supply, drains, sewer connections, backflow devices and gas piping work done by a licensed gas fitter. We do not handle fire sprinkler systems or air conditioning, and we will say so rather than guess.
