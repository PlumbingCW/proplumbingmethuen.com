While you wait: five things to do before we arrive

Safe steps for a heater that is leaking or cold

These are the only steps we ask homeowners to take. Everything else waits for the plumber.

  • Close the cold supply valve on the pipe above the heater to stop water feeding a leak
  • For an electric heater, switch off its breaker at the panel, but only from dry ground
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call Eversource or 911 from outside
  • Put a bucket or wet vac at the edge of any spill and move stored items off the floor
  • Keep children and pets away from hot pipes and wet floors

Do not keep resetting a breaker that trips over and over, and do not relight a gas pilot unless the label instructions walk you through it and there is no gas smell. If it will not stay lit, call. Repeated tries hide the real fault and can make it worse. If the heater still will not behave, write down what you saw and call the 24/7 line.

Water leak

Leaking tank or leaking fitting? The difference decides everything

A leaking fitting, flexible connector, drain valve, or relief valve is a repair. A leak out of the tank shell is not. Water that appears at the bottom with the insulation soaked, or seeps from seams in the jacket, means the liner has corroded through.

Usually repairable

  • Drip at the hot or cold connection on top
  • Wet drain valve at the lower side
  • Relief valve dripping with the tank dry
  • Loose or corroded supply connector

Usually means replacement

  • Water coming from under the tank body
  • Rust streaks down the jacket
  • Puddle that returns after the fitting is dry
  • Tank old and leaking in more than one spot

If the verdict is the tank, our crews move straight to water heater replacement so you are not paying for two visits. Call (603) 815-9179 if you are not sure which side your leak falls on.

Heating problems

No hot water, or not hot enough

Lukewarm water, short hot showers, or nothing but cold are the usual calls we get on tanks. The cause depends on the fuel, so the first question is whether the heater is gas or electric. Both fail in predictable ways.

Gas: pilot or igniter

A pilot that will not stay lit, or an igniter that clicks without lighting the burner, points to a worn thermocouple, igniter, or gas control valve. Gas controls are licensed work.

Electric: element or thermostat

An electric tank has an upper and lower element, each with a thermostat. When one fails, you get a short supply of hot water or none at all. Power must be off before anyone opens a panel.

Tripped limit switch

A high-limit switch cuts power or gas if the water overheats. If it keeps tripping, there is a reason, and resetting it again and again is not the fix.

Sediment and scale

A layer of sediment at the bottom insulates the heating surface and wastes energy. Popping or rumbling sounds are the clue. A flush can help if the tank is sound.

Diagnosis

How we find the fault

1

Read the label and history

We check the rating label for fuel type, capacity, and the manufacture date in the serial number, and ask when the problem began.

2

Test power or gas supply

With a meter for electric units and the controls for gas, the plumber checks whether the heating source is actually reaching the tank.

3

Test the parts

Elements, thermostats, igniters, thermocouples, and the relief valve are checked one at a time rather than replaced by guesswork.

4

Check the tank

The plumber looks for rust at the base, soaked insulation, and wet fittings to confirm the tank can be saved.

Water quality

Hard water, sediment, and flushing

Minerals settle in the bottom of a tank and shorten its life. We do not publish a hardness number for Methuen because we have not verified one. Ask the Methuen Water Department for its annual water quality report, or test your tap with a simple kit, and tell the plumber the result.

Methuen draws its water from the Merrimack River and treats it with a corrosion inhibitor, as the water department's 2023 report describes. Whatever the hardness, a yearly look at the anode rod and a flush are cheap insurance. For tankless owners, the schedule is different and is covered on our tankless page.
