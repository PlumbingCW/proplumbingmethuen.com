Boiler Losing Pressure or No Heat in Methuen, MA

If your boiler has no heat or the pressure gauge keeps dropping, start with three safe checks: the thermostat call for heat, the power switch and breaker, and the pressure gauge reading against what your manufacturer manual lists as normal. If those look fine, bleeding a radiator or checking the zone valve may help.

Stop and call a professional if you smell gas, see water leaking from the boiler, hear banging, or the pressure falls again after a reset. A gas smell means leave the house and call 911 and Eversource from outside.

Safety first: gas smell and carbon monoxide

If you smell gas, leave

Do not flip switches, use a phone inside, or look for the source. Walk out, then call 911 and Eversource from outside or from a neighbor's home. Gas appliance work is licensed work in Massachusetts, so do not try to fix a gas leak or a gas valve yourself.

Carbon monoxide has no smell. A boiler that is sooting, running with a yellow flame, or short-cycling can produce it. Have working CO alarms near sleeping areas and replace the batteries on a schedule.

If a CO alarm sounds, get everyone outside into fresh air and call 911. Do not go back in until the fire department says it is clear. After that, have the boiler checked by a licensed professional before you use it again.

Step by step

Safe checks to run in order

1

Confirm the thermostat is calling

Set it a few degrees above room temperature on heat mode. Replace dead batteries. If the display is blank, the problem may be the thermostat itself.

2

Check power

Most boilers have a switch near the unit that looks like a light switch. Make sure it is on. Check the breaker for the boiler circuit. Reset a tripped breaker once, not repeatedly.

3

Read the pressure gauge

With the system cold, look at the gauge. Your manufacturer manual gives the normal range and the maximum. If it is below the low mark, there may be a leak or the system needs a plumber to add water.

4

Bleed air from radiators

This applies to hot water systems with baseboard or radiators, not steam. With the boiler off, open the bleeder valve slowly with the proper key and close it when water appears. Check the gauge afterward.

5

Look at the zone and reset

If your home has zone valves or multiple thermostats, check each one. Press the reset button once if your manual allows it. If the boiler locks out again, stop.

This is a general guide, not a diagnosis. Follow the instructions printed for your own boiler.

A boiler loses pressure when water leaves the sealed loop. It can leak from a valve, a pipe joint, the relief valve, or the expansion tank. It can also happen after bleeding a radiator.

Pressure that creeps up is a different problem. A waterlogged expansion tank or a leaking fill valve can push the gauge toward the relief setting, and that valve may then discharge water. Do not plug or cap it.

Do it yourself or call a plumber

Safe for a homeowner

  • Raising the thermostat and replacing its batteries
  • Checking the switch and the breaker
  • Reading the pressure gauge and noting it
  • Bleeding a radiator on a hot water system
  • Keeping the area around the boiler clear

Leave to a licensed plumber

  • Anything involving gas piping, valves, or the burner
  • Repeated lockouts or a boiler that will not stay lit
  • Pressure that keeps falling or rising
  • Water leaking from the boiler, pipes, or relief valve
  • Noises such as banging or rumbling

Why gas work is licensed in Massachusetts

In Massachusetts, gas piping and fitting is licensed work. The Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters licenses plumbers and gas fitters, and you can verify a licence on mass.gov.

The reason is simple. A leak, a bad flue connection, or a wrong pressure setting can cause fire or carbon monoxide. If someone offers to adjust a gas boiler without a licence, say no and ask to see it.

Your owner's manual is the standard for the right pressure range, since models differ. Gas fitters are licensed by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, and mass.gov has a licence lookup to confirm a name before work.

Cost factors for a no-heat or low-pressure call

What changes the price

The cause matters most. A thermostat or a reset is a short visit, while a leaking tank, a failed circulator, or a heat exchanger problem can mean parts and extra time.

The city issued an alert about Eversource replacing cast iron and bare steel gas mains in Methuen neighborhoods in 2025. If you hear about street work near you, ask Eversource whether your street is affected, and know that Eversource is also the number to call for a gas smell.

The state residential building code table uses 48 inches as the frost depth for design. Practical consequence: exposed pipes, outdoor drains, and unheated spaces near a boiler can freeze before the ground does. Keep the system running in cold snaps and insulate pipes in garages and crawl spaces.

Gas boiler repair involving fittings, regulators, or valves is licensed gas fitter work in Massachusetts, so verify the licence before you let anyone start.
