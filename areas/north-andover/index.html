Water

Soft water: 35.1 ppm and what it does to your pipes

North Andover's 2025 water quality report lists hardness at 35.1 ppm as calcium carbonate, with a range of 34.4 to 36.4. That is soft. The town draws from Lake Cochichewick, a 2.65 mile lake that is the largest in Essex County, and treats it at its plant on Great Pond Road.

Soft water does not leave the thick scale that hard water does, so a rattling water heater or a crusted showerhead here is rarely a hardness problem. Because soft water can be corrosive to metal, the town adds zinc orthophosphate to protect pipes, and the pH runs around 7.4.

Lead in older homes

The 2023 sampling found 2 of 30 homes above the 0.015 ppm lead action level, with no violation reported. The town says it is removing lead pipes but cannot control the plumbing inside your house. If your home is older, a plumber can check the solder and fittings for you.

Cold season

Winter: 48 inches of frost and what to do when a pipe bursts at 2 a.m.

The state building code table lists a 48 inch frost depth. Exterior walls, garages and the unheated corners of older colonials freeze long before the ground reaches that depth, and a split copper line can dump gallons a minute once it thaws.

Warm season

Late summer: low lake, outdoor ban and irrigation backflow

On September 15, 2026 the town put a mandatory outdoor water ban in place because Lake Cochichewick was low. Under Town Bylaw Chapter 185, spray irrigation, car washing and pool filling are prohibited until the lake recovers. Check the town alert for current status, since indoor plumbing is unaffected.

Summer peak demand reaches 6.5 to 7.0 million gallons a day against a 3.5 million average. A leaking outdoor spigot or sprinkler zone wastes water the town is asking residents to save, so fix it before the ban lifts.

The town's cross-connection program has 368 registered backflow devices and names residential irrigation and home fire systems as its next concern. If you have a lawn system, a backflow device keeps lawn water from flowing back into your drinking supply. Have it tested before the season, not after a complaint.

Storms

Flash floods near Cochichewick Brook and what the sewer does next

In August 2023, six inches of rain fell in six hours, with three inches in two hours, flooding the Davis and Furber Mill Complex area. The town's flood study page describes it as major damage to homes and businesses. Cochichewick Brook runs 1.6 miles from Stevens Pond through East Mill, Osgood and Sutton ponds to the Merrimack.

If your basement sits close to the brook, a working sump pump and a battery backup matter. Our sump pump repair page covers testing and replacement, and we answer storm calls day and night.

North Andover's Water & Sewer Department collects wastewater through 20 pump stations in three drainage areas, East Side, West Side and Central. The Greater Lawrence Sanitary District, whose main facility is here in North Andover, treats it. A pump station outage or heavy rain can slow the flow, so if sewage appears in a floor drain, stop using water and call us. Our crews also cover Lawrence and Andover next door.

Housing

Mill villages, colonials and newer subdivisions

Census data counts 1,728 of 12,115 homes, 14.3%, built in 1939 or earlier. The median year built is 1979. So North Andover is mostly postwar and later, with older pockets.

Machine Shop Village dates to the 1850s and the town Building Department even has a Certificate to Alter form for it. If you own a house there, ask the Building Department before exterior changes.

Rules

Gas, permits and inspections

The town's Building Department at 120 Main Street handles plumbing, gas fitting and sewer permits under the Commonwealth's uniform plumbing and gas code. A final inspection is done for the Certificate of Occupancy. Gas piping and appliance reconnection need a permit and a gas inspection.

Eversource is the gas utility. After the September 2018 Merrimack Valley incident, the town set up a page for appliance restoration. For any gas smell, leave the house and call Eversource or 911 before anyone else.

Gas work in Massachusetts is licensed work, so according to state standards only a licensed gas fitter should touch gas piping, and you can verify a licence on mass.gov.
