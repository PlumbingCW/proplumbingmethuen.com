Housing age

Over 44% of the homes here predate 1940

The Census figures for Lawrence are blunt. Of 31,845 housing units, 44.4% were built in 1939 or earlier, and the citywide median build year is 1950. That means a lot of original pipe is still inside walls, under slabs and in the street connection.

Age shows up as galvanized steel choked with rust, cast iron drains that have flaked inside, and lead or unknown-material water services. When one of those fails at night, it is an emergency, and our 24/7 line is built for it.

Water service

Lead, unknown and copper: where Lawrence service lines stand

Lawrence draws from the Merrimack River and treats it at its own plant on Water Street. The Water & Sewer Department reports 12,777 service lines in the city. Since 2017 it has replaced 126 lead lines, records show about 57 possibly still in place, and 345 are of unknown material.

Those two leftover groups matter to you. In the 2026 report, the 30 homes sampled came in at 0.000 mg/L at the 90th percentile, but the pipe in your own yard is a separate question.

You own the pipe from the street main through the meter

The City states that the service runs from the main up to and including your meter and that the owner pays to maintain it. Since January 2014 new lead cannot go into a service, so lead services are not repaired. The City encourages replacing them with copper. Check the City's service line inventory map for your address, then call a licensed plumber for the replacement.

Sewer and flooding

Combined sewers, the Spicket, and basements that fill in a storm

Lawrence runs about 137 miles of sanitary and combined sewers. Roughly 30% of the system has been separated, so combined pipes still carry both storm runoff and household waste through the older parts of the city. The Greater Lawrence Sanitary District permit lists 6 combined sewer overflow outfalls, and the same district treats the flow in North Andover.

In a hard rain, that shared pipe can fill faster than it drains. Water then looks for the lowest opening, which is your basement floor drain. A backwater valve on the building drain and a working sump pump are the practical defenses.

The Arlington lesson

Arlington sits beside the Spicket River, and it has flooded. In 2003 FEMA, with HUD funds, paid to relocate 22 owners and tenants there, which it called the largest such acquisition in New England. If your basement stays damp near the river, a sump pump check before storm season costs far less than a flooded finish.

Report a sewer overflow to the City at 978-620-3580, then call us if sewage is inside your house.

When a sewer line itself has collapsed or cracked, the fix is excavation or lining, and our page on sewer line repair explains how that decision gets made. Crews that cover Lawrence also take calls from North Andover on its east side and Andover to the south, all through the same dispatch.

Winter

Cold snaps, meters and frozen pipes

The state building code table uses a 48 inch frost depth, and the City itself warns that pipes and meters freeze in attics, basements, crawl spaces, garages and exterior walls. It suggests keeping the thermostat at 55 F or higher and finding your master shutoff before you need it.

Because the service through the meter is yours, a frozen meter or split pipe is your repair. If a pipe bursts in the middle of the night, we treat it as an emergency call, and our page on burst pipe repair covers what we do on site.

Before the first hard freeze

  • Find the main shutoff and turn it once to make sure it moves
  • Insulate pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls in a cold snap
  • Keep the thermostat at 55 F or higher if the house is empty
Gas

Gas appliances in South Lawrence and everywhere else

After the September 13, 2018 over-pressurization of the Columbia Gas system, which hit Lawrence, Andover and North Andover, gas piping and appliance hookups in many homes were disconnected, abandoned or relit. Eversource now operates the gas system.

A licensed plumber and gas fitter should confirm that your water heater has a correct vent, a shutoff and a sound connection. If you smell gas, leave the house and call Eversource or 911 first. Do not call a plumber first.
