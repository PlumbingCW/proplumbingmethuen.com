Neighborhoods and utilities

Dracut by village, and who supplies your water

Dracut is a town of villages: Dracut Center around Town Hall on Arlington Street, East Dracut toward the Methuen line, Kenwood, the old mill villages of Navy Yard and Collinsville, and the Lakeview Avenue area. Our 24/7 emergency dispatch covers all of them from Methuen, so a burst line in East Dracut at midnight gets the same call-out as one at noon.

Which utility you deal with depends on where you live. The Dracut Water Supply District is separate from town government and serves most of town. The Kenwood Water District, a town department, serves the eastern side. A few homes run on private wells. Knowing which one feeds your house tells you who to call to shut off water at the street.

Navy Yard and Collinsville

Mill-village housing here dates from roughly 1895 to 1938, including tenement houses and worker housing around Mill Street. Homes of that age often have old supply lines, so test your shutoff valve before you need it.

Lakeview Avenue area

Lots here were created in 1897 after the streetcar arrived, and Beaver Brook crosses the corridor. Basement water near Beaver Brook is the local risk.

East Dracut and Kenwood

The eastern side is where Kenwood Water serves homes with water bought from Methuen and Lowell. Pressure or taste questions go to the district first, then to us if the problem is inside your walls.

Dracut Center

The town hall area mixes older houses with newer ones. A slow leak is easy to miss, so watch for damp spots and a meter that keeps moving with everything off.

Emergency

Burst pipe or active leak in Dracut: before we arrive

Whoever supplies your water, the first five minutes are the same. These steps keep a bad leak from becoming a ruined floor while a licensed plumber is on the way.

Shut the main valve

Turn the main shutoff inside your house clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, do not force it. For a curb or street problem, call the Dracut Water Supply District at 978-957-0441, or Kenwood Water at 978-957-0371 if you are on the eastern side.

Kill power near the water

If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, switch off the breaker only if you can reach it without stepping in water. Otherwise stay out and tell us when you call.

Open a tap low in the house

With the main off, run a cold tap on the lowest floor to drain the line and relieve pressure. Leave the hot taps alone if the heater is the source.

Call the 24/7 line

Call (603) 815-9179 with your street, where the water is and whether it is still running. Move rugs, electronics and stored boxes off the wet area while you wait.

Cold season

Winter in Dracut: freezing pipes and hose bibs

The state building code table uses a 48 inch frost depth, and Dracut's own hazard mitigation plan ranks snowstorms and extreme cold first among the town's top hazards. That combination is why frozen and split pipes are a seasonal call here. Now is the time to deal with the weak spots before the first hard freeze.

Pipes freeze where cold gets in: outside walls, unheated garages, crawlspaces, and exterior hose bibs with a hose still attached.

Before the first hard freeze

  • Disconnect hoses and shut the inside valve feeding each outdoor spigot
  • Open cabinet doors on outside walls under kitchen and bath sinks on very cold nights
  • Let a cold tap drip on a line you know has frozen before
  • Seal gaps where pipes pass through the foundation or rim joist
  • Know where your main shutoff is and that it still turns

Do not pull your water meter to winterize

The Dracut Water Supply District seals its meters and says customers must not remove them, even to winterize. If you need a meter pulled for the season, call the district at 978-957-0441 to schedule it. Tampering carries a penalty.

If a pipe has already frozen and no water is coming out, shut the main and open the tap. Do not use a torch. If a pipe has split, you will see water as it thaws, and that is a burst pipe repair job we handle on the emergency line. For a no-heat call in a cold snap, our plumbers also work on boiler problems.

Flooding

Brooks, ponds and basements: when the water comes from outside

Beaver Brook runs through the west side of Dracut and topped its banks in the May 2006 flood, flooding the Lakeview Avenue bridge. The town's hazard plan also lists the shorelines of Lake Mascuppic, Peters Pond, Cedar Pond and Long Pond, plus Peppermint Brook and Bridge Street, among flood-prone areas. The Merrimack River forms much of the southern border, and spring snowmelt with rain on frozen ground is its main flood trigger.

For a homeowner near any of those, the plumbing consequence is simple: your sump pump, its check valve and your floor drains carry the load. A pump that sat idle all summer is the one that fails on the first big storm.

Check yourself before storm season

  • Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and watch the pump start
  • Look for a discharge pipe that runs away from the house
  • Listen for a pump that hums but does not move water

Call us for

  • A pump that will not start or keeps cycling
  • Water standing in the pit after the storm has passed
  • A check valve that bangs or lets water drain back
  • Floor drains that gurgle or back up in heavy rain

Our team handles sump pump repair day and night, and a backed-up floor drain during a storm qualifies as an emergency in our book. The town's Building Department can also tell you whether your property sits in a flood plain, which is worth asking before you spend on waterproofing.

Water quality

Rusty water, new mains and what the district is doing

The Dracut Water Supply District describes its water as soft and treats iron and manganese, which it says can still turn water rusty or black for some customers, especially after main flushing. After a flushing round, run the cold tap until the water clears and check a white load before you start laundry.

Sediment also collects in water heaters. If your tank rumbles or you see rust in hot water only, that points at the heater rather than the main, and water heater repair is the right call.

The district is also finishing a water main project on Lakeview Avenue, Lawrence Road, Christine Avenue, Makos Street and nearby streets, scheduled to finish by November 15, 2026, with final paving in spring 2027. Street work can bring temporary shutoffs and pressure swings. Afterward, if a faucet screen clogs or a fixture sputters, that sediment may have come with the work.

Sewer and wells

Sewer, septic and the town side of the pipe

Roughly 95 percent of Dracut's population is on municipal sewer, according to the town's 2020 hazard mitigation plan. Part of the flow goes to Lowell's regional wastewater utility, and part goes through Methuen to the Greater Lawrence Sanitary District. For a backup that affects the street, the Dracut Sewer Department keeps an emergency line at 978-957-2123.

Homes on Jones Avenue, Methuen Road, Island Pond Road and the northern part of Marsh Hill Road are noted as on septic. Those homes may have well pumps and pressure tanks, so a loss of pressure can be a pump issue rather than a main issue.
