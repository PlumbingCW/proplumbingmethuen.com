Sewer Camera Inspection Cost in Methuen, MA: What Moves the Price

A sewer camera inspection sends a waterproof camera on a flexible rod through your main drain line and records what it sees: cracks, roots, offsets, sags and buildup. The price depends on access, the length and condition of the line, whether cleaning must come first, and whether you need the problem located and documented.

A straightforward visit with a clear cleanout costs far less than one that needs the line cleared, the camera pulled from the roof vent, and a locate on the ground above. Our licensed plumbers quote a clear price before work starts, so call (603) 815-9179 and describe your setup.

The service

What you are paying for

The camera itself is only part of the visit. You are paying for a trained operator who can read the picture, a clean enough pipe to see anything, and a record you can keep. A camera pushed through a full pipe shows you grease and nothing else.

That is why two quotes for a camera inspection can look very different. One may include cleaning and a written report, the other may only promise a quick look. Ask what is included before you compare prices. Our sewer inspection visits spell out the scope up front.

Cost factors

The five factors that set the price

Access to the line

A cleanout outside the house is the easy case. If the only way in is through a toilet flange or a roof vent, setup takes longer and the visit costs more.

Length and depth of the line

A short lateral to the street is faster than a long run to a distant connection. Depth affects locating as well.

Cleaning before the camera

If the line is clogged, it has to be cleared first with a cable or a water jet. That is real work in addition to the inspection.

Locating and marking

Finding a defect on the surface so you know where to dig adds equipment and time. Some visits only need a depth reading.

Report and recording

A saved video and a written summary take extra time. They are valuable if you are buying, selling or filing a claim.

Process

How the inspection goes

1

Find the entry point

The plumber locates a cleanout or a pull-out fixture and confirms the line is open enough to run a camera.

2

Clear if needed

A cable, or a jetter for heavy buildup, clears the pipe so the lens sees the pipe wall.

3

Run the camera

The head travels toward the street while a monitor shows the pipe. The operator notes anything unusual and the distance from the entry.

4

Locate the problem

A transmitter in the camera head lets the crew find the exact surface point and depth of a defect.

5

Review and decide

You see the footage, hear the plain description and get repair options with the trade-offs.

Choose

Camera only, or camera plus repair planning

A basic camera look fits when

  • You want to confirm a clog source
  • The line is short and accessible
  • You are checking a house before buying
  • No symptoms yet, just a check on an aging line

Plan for more when

  • There are repeated backups
  • You smell sewer gas or see a damp spot
  • Roots or a sag are likely
  • You need a location for excavation or repair pricing

When the footage shows a break, offset or root intrusion, the next step is repair. Our sewer line repair team can walk you through spot repair, relining and replacement options using the recording as the reference.

Safety

When to call and what to avoid

  • If you feel dizzy, sick or have a headache, get everyone outside and call 911 before calling us.
  • Do not pour drain cleaners into a line you plan to inspect. They can harm skin and equipment.
  • Do not run water continuously into a backing-up line.
  • Do not dig around a sewer line without locating it first.
  • Do not hire anyone for repair work who cannot show a Massachusetts plumbing license.

Methuen's combined sewer and who handles repairs

Part of Methuen's sewer system is combined, carrying stormwater and wastewater together, and the Greater Lawrence Sanitary District in North Andover treats the flow. The district's draft federal permit lists five combined sewer overflow outfalls, four to the Merrimack River and one to the Spicket River. A camera inspection tells you the condition of your own line, whatever happens downstream when storms load the shared system.

Plumbing work in Massachusetts needs a permit issued to a licensed plumber, and Methuen's Inspections Division issues plumbing permits. If the camera finds a defect that needs repair, your licensed plumber applies for the permit.

Work is not supposed to be covered up before it is inspected, so ask when the inspection happens. The 2022 fee schedule lists a $50 minimum, a $50 capped sewer line fee and a $30 pre-sewer entry permit. Check the current fees with the Inspections Division at 978-983-8625.

If your plan goes beyond a look into the pipe, ask the Inspections Division which permit applies before work starts.
