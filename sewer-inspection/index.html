Sewer Inspection in Methuen, MA

A sewer inspection is a camera look inside the pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the street, so problems can be seen instead of guessed. Licensed Methuen plumbers push a waterproof camera through your line and record what it finds.

It is a planned visit, and the footage tells you whether you need cleaning, repair or nothing at all.

What a camera inspection shows

Pipes hide problems. A camera shows the pipe material, the joints, the slope and any blockage, with a live view on a monitor. You can see roots entering a joint, a section that has sagged, or a crack running along the pipe.

For older Methuen homes, where the line may be cast iron, clay or something else, the camera also identifies what the pipe is made of. That affects every decision after it, from cleaning method to repair options.

The equipment we use

Push camera

A flexible rod with a camera head slides through a cleanout or access point and travels along the line.

Self-leveling head

The image stays upright even as the camera rolls through bends, so you read the pipe correctly.

Locator

A transmitter in the camera head can show its position above ground and depth, so repairs can be planned.

Recording

Ask the plumber for the footage so you can review it, show a contractor or keep it for your records.

Timing

When to schedule an inspection

  • Before buying an older home, especially one with mature trees near the line
  • After a second or third backup in the same year
  • Before landscaping, a deck or a driveway over the sewer path
  • After a sewer cleaning, to confirm the pipe is intact
  • When a basement smells of sewer with no visible cause

If your line has clogged repeatedly, see our sewer line cleaning page. An inspection afterward shows whether cleaning was enough. Repairs that come out of an inspection follow Massachusetts plumbing code (248 CMR) standards, and permits go through a licensed plumber.

If the camera finds grease or scale, hydro jetting is often the fix. If it finds a failed section, our sewer line repair page explains the options.

What a camera cannot show

A camera sees the inside of the pipe it can reach. It cannot see through a fully collapsed section or past a hard blockage, and it cannot tell you what is happening in the soil around the pipe.

It also does not predict when an old pipe will fail. It gives you the condition today. A clean pipe can still be at risk if it is thin or very old, and we will say so when it looks that way.

Any repair that follows an inspection is carried out under the Massachusetts plumbing code (248 CMR), so keep the camera findings handy as a record of the pipe's condition.
