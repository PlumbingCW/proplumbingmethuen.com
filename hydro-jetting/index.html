Emergency Hydro Jetting in Methuen, MA

Hydro jetting is a drain cleaning method that sends high-pressure water through a hose and nozzle to scour grease, scale and roots off the whole pipe wall. If a drain is backing up and a cable did not fix it, we are on 24/7 emergency dispatch: call (603) 815-9179.

Jetting is not the first tool for every clog. It is the right one when the blockage keeps returning or a snake could not get through.

Why jet

When a snake stalled or the clog keeps returning

A cable drills a hole through a clog. If the pipe is coated with grease or heavy scale, that hole closes again in weeks, and you call for the third time. Jetting removes the coating itself, so the pipe is the size it was meant to be.

The snake cleared it, then it came back

That pattern points to buildup on the pipe wall, not a single object. Jetting addresses the wall.

The cable would not pass

A dense blockage or a thick root mass can stop a cable. Water pressure and a cutting nozzle can keep going.

Slow drains in several places

Narrowing along the main line shows up as slowness everywhere, not a hard stoppage in one place.

Process

How hydro jetting works

1

Camera first

We run a push camera down the line to see the blockage and the pipe material. Jetting a collapsed or cracked pipe is how small problems become big ones.

2

Pick the nozzle

A forward-facing penetrating nozzle opens a stoppage. A rotating or root-cutting head scours walls and trims roots. Rear-facing jets pull the hose along the pipe.

3

Set the pressure

Pressure goes far above what comes out of your tap, so it is matched to pipe size and condition. Fragile pipe gets a gentler setting or a different method.

4

Flush and recheck

After the pass, we flush the line and run the camera again to confirm the pipe wall is clean.

Most jetting is done through a cleanout, which gives a straight path to the main line. If your house does not have one, we find another access point, such as a toilet removal or a roof vent. Our sewer inspection service uses the same camera and can document the pipe before and after.

Jetting versus snaking

Jetting

  • Cleans the full pipe wall
  • Handles grease and scale that cables leave behind
  • Longer-lasting result on repeat clogs
  • Needs sound pipe

Snaking

  • Opens a hole through the clog
  • Faster for a simple one-time blockage
  • Lower cost for easy jobs
  • Does not remove buildup on the wall

For an everyday hair clog in one tub, a cable is the better tool and jetting would be overkill. For a main line that has stopped twice this year, jetting usually makes more sense. Our drain cleaning page covers the cabling side.

What jetting cannot fix

Water pressure clears debris. It does not repair pipe. If the camera shows a collapsed section, an offset joint or a belly where water pools, jetting may relieve the symptom for a few days, but the problem will return.

When the camera finds damage

We will show you the footage, explain what is wrong and give you a clear price for the repair. See sewer line repair for the options once a pipe has failed.

Risks on old, fragile pipe

Older homes in many towns, Methuen included, can still have original cast iron or clay lines. Ask your plumber to identify yours on camera. Those materials may be thin, cracked or held together by roots. High pressure could widen a crack or wash out surrounding soil.

That is why we look first. If the pipe looks too weak, we switch to a lower setting, use cabling or recommend repair. We would rather lose the jetting job than damage your line.

Jetting pressure is set according to the equipment maker's guidelines for the pipe diameter and material, which is why a fragile cast iron or clay line gets a gentler setting.
