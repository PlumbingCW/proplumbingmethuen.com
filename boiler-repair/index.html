Turn off the water feed valve to the boiler and the boiler power switch. Keep people away from wet electrical areas. A small drip from a relief valve is still a sign of a problem.

Hot water or steam

How a Massachusetts boiler heats your home

Many Massachusetts homes heat with a hot water or steam boiler feeding cast iron radiators or baseboard. In a hot water system, a pump moves heated water through the loops and back. In a steam system, the boiler makes steam that rises on its own and returns as condensate.

The two systems fail differently, so the first job is knowing which you have. A pressure gauge and a small sight glass on the boiler usually settle it. Steam systems show a water level glass, and hot water systems show a pressure gauge.

Hot water boiler

  • Pressure gauge reads about 12 to 15 psi cold on most residential units
  • Needs a circulator and an expansion tank
  • Common problems: air, low pressure, pump or zone valve failure
  • Radiators and baseboard warm evenly

Steam boiler

  • Sight glass shows the water level
  • Common problems: low water, clogged vents, stuck traps
  • Radiators heat from the pipe end out, with a hiss at the vent
  • Needs routine blowdown and level control checks

Pressure, expansion tank and circulator problems

Pressure too low

A hot water system that loses pressure may have a leak or a failed expansion tank. Topping it up repeatedly hides the cause.

Pressure too high

Pressure that climbs past the safe range, or a relief valve that drips, often points to a waterlogged expansion tank.

Circulator not running

If the boiler is hot but radiators are cold, the pump may have failed or seized. A pump is a routine replacement.

Zone valves, air in the lines and bleeding radiators

If one room is cold and the rest are warm, a stuck zone valve or air in that loop is the likely cause. A gurgling radiator or a cold top edge usually means trapped air.

Bleeding a radiator is a safe homeowner step if you do it carefully. It is for hot water systems only, not steam.

1

Turn the heat down and let it cool

Hot water can scald. Wait until the radiator is cool to the touch.

2

Find the bleed valve

It is a small valve at the top corner of a cast iron or baseboard radiator.

3

Open it slightly with a key or screwdriver

Hold a cloth under it. Air hisses out. Close the valve as soon as water appears.

4

Check the boiler pressure

Bleeding lowers pressure. If the gauge dips below the normal range, call a plumber rather than guessing.

Lockouts, ignition and gas boilers

A boiler that will not light, or lights and then shuts off, may have a dirty flame sensor, a failed igniter, a blocked flue or a gas supply issue. Eversource is the gas utility serving Methuen, and the city has issued an alert about Eversource replacing older cast iron and bare steel gas mains, so a street-side gas issue is the utility's to fix.

If the burner tries repeatedly and fails, do not keep resetting it. A licensed plumber can check the controls. Any work on gas piping requires a licensed gas fitter, and we will tell you if the cause is on the utility side.

Boiler repairs follow the manufacturer's service guidelines for your model, and in Massachusetts gas boiler work is licensed work that must meet the state plumbing and gas standards.

Cast iron radiators last for generations, but valves, vents and pipe joints around them age. A radiator that hammers or leaks at the valve stem often needs a repacked or replaced valve, not a new radiator. Frozen pipes are also a risk in Massachusetts winters, especially in unheated basements and exterior walls.

Winter prep and cost factors

Check your boiler before the heating season, not in January when every company is busy. Look for drips, rust at the joints and a pressure gauge that sits where it normally does. Call if anything looks different from last year. If a leak or valve failure requires more, we cover that under our water heater repair and leak detection services as well.
