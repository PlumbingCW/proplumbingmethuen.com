Exterior Basement Waterproofing Membrane in Methuen, MA: How It Works

Exterior waterproofing means digging down along the foundation wall, cleaning and repairing the surface, applying a waterproof membrane, adding drainage board, and moving water away through a footing drain and proper backfill. It stops water at the wall instead of letting it in and catching it inside.

It is also the most disruptive and expensive route. In many basements, an interior drain and sump pump handle the same problem with less digging. This guide explains both sides and where a plumber fits into the work.

The layers

How an exterior system works

Think of the system as layers. The wall is cleaned and cracks are repaired. A membrane is applied against the wall. A dimpled drainage board sits over it. A drain at the footing carries water away. Clean backfill and sloped grading finish the job.

Membrane

A rubberized or sheet barrier that keeps water from entering the foundation wall.

Drainage board

A dimpled sheet that gives water a path down the wall and protects the membrane from backfill.

Footing drain

A perforated pipe at the base of the foundation that collects water and moves it to a safe outlet.

Backfill and grading

Free-draining fill and a slope away from the house keep roof and surface water from pooling at the wall.

Compare

Membrane versus coating

Common exterior wall treatments
TreatmentWhat it isLimits
Damp-proofing coatingA thin sprayed or rolled layerSlows moisture but may not bridge cracks or resist water pressure
Rubberized membraneA thicker applied waterproof layerNeeds clean, sound wall and careful application
Sheet membraneA manufactured sheet adhered to the wallSeams and penetrations need attention
Membrane plus drainage boardBarrier plus a drainage pathMore involved, handles water pressure better

A coating on its own is a thin barrier. If the soil is holding water against the wall, a barrier without a drain asks the wall to resist pressure forever. That is why the drainage layers matter as much as the membrane.

Steps

What happens on site

1

Locate utilities and plan

Water, sewer and gas lines are marked before anyone digs. Permit questions are settled before the first shovel.

2

Excavate to the footing

The soil along the wall is dug out, often with an excavator, down to the footing. This is the loud and messy part.

3

Clean, repair and apply

The wall is cleaned, cracks and tie holes are repaired, and the membrane is applied. Structural cracks are repaired by the right trade, not by the membrane.

4

Add drainage board and footing drain

The drainage board goes on, the footing drain is laid in washed stone, and it is connected to an outlet or sump.

5

Backfill and regrade

Soil is returned in layers, and the final grade slopes away from the foundation. Downspouts are extended.

Decision

When interior drainage is the better answer

Exterior makes sense when

  • The wall itself is cracking or leaking over a large area
  • Access around the house is open
  • The foundation needs repairs from outside
  • You want the wall protected from the soil side

Interior makes sense when

  • Water comes up at the floor-wall joint
  • Decks, patios or neighbors block excavation
  • Budget or disruption is a concern
  • A sump pump setup can handle the volume

An interior perimeter drain and sump pump collect water that has already reached the footing and send it out. It does not keep the wall dry, but it keeps the floor dry, and it is a plumbing job we do. See sump pump repair for the pump side.

Scope

What a plumber covers and what other trades do

Our side is the drainage: footing drains, sump pits, pumps, discharge lines and the plumbing connections. Structural foundation repair, concrete work, excavation and masonry belong to other trades. A good project names who does what before it starts.

The membrane maker's installation guidelines are the standard for seams, laps and edge termination, so ask to see them. The sump and discharge piping part of the job falls under the Massachusetts plumbing code (248 CMR) and belongs with a licensed plumber.

  • Plumber: drain pipe, sump, pump, discharge, check valve
  • Excavator or foundation contractor: digging, wall repair, membrane in many cases
  • Landscaper or grader: final slope and downspout extensions

Do not DIY excavation next to a foundation. A deep trench can collapse, and removing support from the wall can cause damage.

Frost, flooding and permits in Methuen

The statewide residential code table uses a 48 inch frost depth for design. Exterior work that goes down to a footing should be planned with that depth in mind, since the soil around the wall can freeze and heave.

Properties near those areas deserve extra attention to where the water goes.
