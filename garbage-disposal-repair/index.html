Urgent

Leaking, burning smell or will not stop: urgent signs

Leaking under the sink

Water in the cabinet can come from the disposal housing, the sink flange or the drain. Put a bowl under it, stop using the sink and shut off the unit.

Burning smell or smoke

Turn the switch off and shut off the breaker. Do not run it again. A burning smell can mean a seized motor or a wire problem.

Will not stop running

A switch that sticks on needs power cut at the breaker. Do not reach into the unit to stop it.

Sink full of dirty water

A disposal that no longer drains is often jammed, or the line past it is blocked. Stop the sink and call.

Do it carefully

Reset and hex key steps: the safe version

Many jams clear in two minutes, but only with the power off. Never put your hand into the disposal, even when it seems dead. Use tongs or pliers for anything you can see.

1

Switch off the power

Turn off the wall switch. Then shut off the breaker for the disposal if you can, or unplug the unit if it has a plug under the sink.

2

Use the hex key from below

Most disposals have a hex socket in the center of the bottom. Fit the wrench that came with the unit, or a matching hex key, and work it back and forth until the plate turns freely.

3

Remove debris from above with tongs

Use a flashlight and pliers or tongs. Do not use your fingers.

4

Press the red reset button

On the bottom of the unit is a small red button. Press it, restore power and test with a little water running.

If it trips again or hums again

Stop. Repeat tripping means a failing motor or a deeper issue. Leave power off and call us.

Decision

Repair or replace the disposal?

Repair or reset when

  • The cause is a jam
  • The housing is dry and sound
  • Power and switch work normally

Replace when

  • The housing leaks from the side or bottom
  • The motor hums, trips and stalls repeatedly
  • Rust or corrosion shows on the unit

A leaking housing cannot be patched for good, and a motor that keeps stalling rarely recovers. Size matters too: a bigger motor handles more, but the unit has to fit the space under your sink.

Replacement

Installing a disposal: the basics

Installation starts with power off, then the old unit is released from its mounting ring and the drain is disconnected. The sink flange is cleaned and sealed, the new mounting assembly is fitted, and the disposal is hung and aligned to the drain.

The dishwasher drain hose connects to a small inlet on the disposal. On a new unit, the plug inside that inlet must be knocked out before the hose is connected, or the dishwasher will not drain. It is a common miss on a first install.

The electrical connection is either a plug or a hardwired box with a switch. If a new circuit or switch is needed, a licensed electrician handles that part. Manufacturer instructions govern every install.

A new unit is mounted and plumbed according to the manufacturer's installation instructions, and any wiring is done by a licensed electrician. Any new circuit or hardwired box should meet the electrical standards that apply to the home.

Prevention

What not to grind, plus a note on sewer and septic

Keep these out of the disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat, which coat the pipe and harden
  • Coffee grounds in quantity
  • Fibrous food such as celery, corn husks and artichoke
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell
  • Bones, fruit pits and shells
  • Non-food items

If your home is on a septic system, ask the manufacturer of your tank or your septic pro about disposals before installing one. Disposal waste adds solids to the system. On city sewer, the same grease and fiber rules apply, and what you send down can add to sewer clogs. See sewer line cleaning if kitchen drains back up repeatedly.

Tie-in

Kitchen sink clog or disposal problem?

The disposal sits in the middle of your kitchen drain, so a kitchen clog can look like a disposal failure. If the unit spins but the sink still drains slowly, the clog is likely in the line past it or in the shared trap with the dishwasher.

That is a drain job, not a motor problem. Our drain cleaning work clears these, and a camera shows whether grease buildup or something else is the cause.
