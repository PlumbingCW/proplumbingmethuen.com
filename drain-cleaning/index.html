Drain backing up right now? Do this first

A drain that is overflowing or gurgling is telling you the line is blocked somewhere downstream. The next ten minutes decide whether you mop a bathroom or tear out a floor.

1

Stop all water use

No flushing, no dishwasher, no washing machine. Each one sends more water toward the blockage.

2

Check how many drains are affected

One slow sink points to a local clog. A toilet, tub and floor drain all acting up together points to the main drain line.

3

Protect the area

Move rugs and boxes off the floor. If water is near an outlet, power strip or appliance, turn off that circuit at the breaker panel and stay out of the standing water.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Tell the dispatcher which fixtures are backing up and where the water is going. We schedule same-day or after-hours visits when a drain is actively overflowing.

Snaking or jetting: which one clears your drain

Drain snaking, also called cabling, pushes a rotating steel cable through the pipe to bore a hole through the clog or snag it. It is the fastest fix for a single blockage like a hairball or a wad of paper.

Drain snaking (cabling)

  • Opens a path through the clog
  • Quick, good for one-time blockages
  • Gentle on most pipe
  • May leave a coat of grease on pipe walls

Hydro jetting

  • Scours the full pipe wall with high-pressure water
  • Best for grease, scale and repeat clogs
  • Needs a sound pipe to be safe
  • Often paired with a camera look first

If the same drain clogs every few months, the cable is probably only drilling a hole through a buildup that keeps regrowing. That is when hydro jetting earns its place.

Cable heads and cutters are used according to the tool maker's guidelines for the pipe size and material, so a fragile old line is not forced past what it can take.

Kitchen sink drain clogs and grease

Kitchen clogs are different from bathroom clogs. Cooking fat goes down warm and liquid, then cools and coats the inside of the pipe. Food scraps, coffee grounds and starchy rice stick to that coating, and the opening shrinks a little each week.

Slow drain for weeks

The pipe is partly narrowed. Clearing it now is cheaper than clearing a full stoppage later.

Dishwasher water in the sink

The dishwasher drains into the same line. A backup there means the clog is past the sink trap.

Smell from the drain

Rotting food films on the pipe wall make the odor. Cleaning the wall, not just opening the pipe, fixes it.

If a garbage disposal is installed and the sink still will not drain, the blockage may be beyond it. See our disposal repair page if the unit itself hums or jams.

What not to do to a clogged drain

Skip the chemical drain openers

Liquid openers sit in standing water and can leave a caustic pool that splashes on whoever works on the drain next. They also soften some old pipe and rarely clear a full stoppage. If you already poured one, tell the plumber what it was.

Also avoid

  • Running a plunger on a sink with an open overflow hole without blocking it
  • Forcing a long wire or coat hanger down a toilet or tub drain
  • Pouring grease down the drain and following it with hot water, which only moves the fat farther down the line
  • Running the dishwasher or washer to see if it clears on its own
  • Ignoring a drain that backs up again within days

Older cast iron and clay drains in older Methuen homes

Older homes in many towns, Methuen included, can still have original cast iron or clay lines. Ask your plumber to identify yours on camera. Cast iron roughens and scales on the inside as it ages, giving grease and paper something to catch on. Clay pipe comes in short sections whose joints can let tree roots in.

Those materials do not fail on a schedule, but they do change how we clear a drain. We favor a camera look and careful cabling over aggressive tools on pipe that looks fragile, and we tell you honestly if a repair makes more sense than another cleaning. See sewer camera inspection for how that works.

A drain line that leaks

Sometimes the problem is not a clog but a joint or crack letting wastewater out. Damp ceiling drywall under a bathroom, a musty smell or a stain on a basement wall can all come from a leaking drain line. Cleaning will not fix it. Locating and repairing the leak will.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

Until we arrive

  • Keep every faucet, toilet and appliance off
  • Locate the cleanout if you have one, usually a capped pipe in the basement or by the foundation
  • Take photos of any water damage for your records
  • Keep children and pets away from wet floors and any water near outlets
  • Tell us if you used a chemical opener
